## Who develops Dawn of War IV?

**KING Art Games** — a German studio with real-time strategy pedigree, best known for **Iron Harvest**, the dieselpunk RTS. Development of Dawn of War IV sits with them; **Deep Silver (PLAION)** publishes, under license from **Games Workshop**, the owners of the Warhammer 40,000 universe. The game launches December 3, 2026 on [PC via Steam](/guides/release-time/).

## Isn't Dawn of War a Relic series?

Historically, yes — **Relic Entertainment** developed Dawn of War I through III, plus Company of War's spiritual siblings like Company of Heroes. For Dawn of War IV the baton passed to KING Art. Long-time fans tracking that handoff will notice it in the game's DNA: KING Art's Iron Harvest background shows in the [unit detail and ultimate units](/guides/ultimate-units/), while the [Blood Ravens campaign heritage](/factions/space-marines/) stays proudly intact.

## Who is on the development team?

Named leads confirmed through official channels:

- **Jan Theysen** — Game Director / Creative Director (seen across the official [Battlefield Reports](/guides/last-stand/))
- **Orvi Thummel** — QA Lead
- **Elliott Verbiest** — Senior Game Designer

## Why does the developer matter for buyers?

Track record predicts post-launch behavior:

- KING Art shipped Iron Harvest with years of updates — relevant when reading the [Year One roadmap](/guides/roadmap/) (two story expansions, free map packs, a Mission Editor)
- Deep Silver / PLAION is a major publisher with established [edition and early-unlock practices](/guides/editions-preorder/)
- Games Workshop licensing means the [four factions](/guides/factions-overview/) and the [Kronus campaign setting](/guides/campaign-overview/) stay true to 40K canon

## What about the old Dawn of War 3 worry?

Fair question — Dawn of War III's abandonment still haunts the community. The difference worth noting: Dawn of War IV arrives with a **published Year One plan** (story DLC, maps, editor, [Crusade mode](/guides/roadmap/)), and KING Art's own history of long-tail support. The [closed beta](/guides/open-beta/) already stress-tested the build ahead of the December 3 launch. Whether the promise holds — we track it here, with dated sources, as it happens.
